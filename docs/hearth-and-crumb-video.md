# Hearth & Crumb — AI video concept

Updated November 25, 2026.

## Purpose and status

A 15–20-second social ad for the fictional Hearth & Crumb bakery, demonstrating Spud Films' AI video work. Intended for Instagram/Facebook Reels, with the finished example also shown on the Spud Films website alongside its fictional source homepage.

Two concepts are below. **Concept 2 (raspberry cream puff) is preferred.** Concept 1 is kept for reference.

---

# Concept 2: Raspberry cream puff (preferred)

Two shots plus an end card, about 15–20 seconds. The craving is the idea; the craft is the hook. No box drop: it has to feel realistic, and a delicate pastry can't survive a crash.

## Shot 1: the cut and pull-back

- **Hook (first 1–2 seconds):** extreme close-up of a knife cutting through a raspberry cream puff (crisp crackled choux shell, whipped vanilla cream, fresh raspberries, raspberry jam center). The crack of the shell is clearly audible.
- Her hands slowly separate the two halves.
- While she does, the camera pulls back to reveal the open white box beside her, logo clean and readable on the lid. The box held four cream puffs; one slot is now empty (the one she's working with).
- About 4–5 seconds, one continuous shot.

## Shot 2: the lift

- New angle from her side. Put the camera on the same side as the hand holding the tart, so her arm doesn't block it.
- She lifts one half toward her mouth with one hand (it must be hand-held, no fork), smiling.
- A single slow bead of raspberry jam rolls over the cut edge. Shallow focus: the drip is sharp, her face soft behind it. Spell out "one slow drip" in the prompt, because AI overdoes liquids.
- Cut just before the bite.

## End card

- Hearth & Crumb logo, the address, and one small line: **We deliver too.** (Explains the box in her kitchen.)
- The **bite sound carries over** the end card: one crisp crunch, not chewing.
- Build it in Premiere: logo from the site image, color matched with the eyedropper, typed address. Every letter guaranteed.

## Sound

- From Veo: knife crack, pastry, room tone, tied to the action.
- **Voiceover: "...just this once."** Her inner voice talking herself into it (everyone says it, everyone knows it's a lie — that's the charm), landing on her pause just before the bite. Airy and intimate, just above a whisper; dry, no reverb. Then a beat of silence, the crunch, the logo. Don't say the bakery's name; the logo shows it.
- Voiceover from Google's AI voices (supports emotion/delivery direction). Music bed added separately in Premiere.

## Look

- Warm white kitchen (not stark white), cream counter, a touch of light wood, sparse background, soft morning window light from the left.
- Cream puffs about 3 inches across, so half fits easily in one hand.

## Production

1. Nano Banana reference images first: kitchen, cream puff (whole), cream puff (cut open, made from the whole one), box (with the logo attached), actress. Reuse them for every frame.
2. For each shot, make the start and end frames as stills in Nano Banana, then have Veo (in Flow) animate between them. Make the end frame from the start frame so pastry, light and counter match.
3. Assemble in Premiere: two clips, end card, carried-over bite sound, music.

## Ideas considered and dropped

- Box falling from the sky: unrealistic, and would wreck the pastry.
- A hand sneaking off with her other half: reads as "what was that?" in a 15-second ad.
- Raspberry tart: shows everything from the top, so the cut isn't a reveal.
- Mirror-glazed entremet / mille-feuille: look great cut, but can't be held in one hand.

# Concept 1: Box landing (earlier idea)

## Opening: the box landing

Start on an empty kitchen counter. A box bearing the Hearth & Crumb logo lands with a firm **thump**, slides about an inch, and the woman's hands steady it. The movement feels like a slightly rough set-down, while the pastries remain beautifully arranged inside.

The sudden motion and isolated impact sound are the opening hook. Start the music just after the thump.

## Reveal and ingredients

A woman is seated at her kitchen counter. She opens the box to reveal four handheld breakfast choices:

- Butter croissant
- Apple fritter
- Buttermilk donut
- Blueberry muffin

A steaming coffee is to her left, coming into view during the reveal rather than occupying the otherwise empty opening counter.

Begin over her shoulder, looking into the box. Use large, bold titles rather than subtitle-style captions.

Working main title: **Great mornings start with great ingredients.**

Working supporting phrases: **Real butter. Whole blueberries. Chunks of apple.**

Working voiceover: *We're particular about what goes into your breakfast.*

The ingredient language is the proposed point of distinction and still needs polishing.

## Choice and anticipation

The camera swings approximately 180 degrees from behind/over her shoulder to the front as she chooses the croissant. Resolve the exact overlap of the camera move and hand action in the mockup.

Finish the camera movement before she lifts the croissant toward her lips. End the action just as it reaches her lips; **no bite or chewing**.

Bold title, potentially behind her: **Your morning deserves this.**

Working voiceover: *Because your morning deserves this.*

## Closing card

- Hearth & Crumb logo
- **Family-owned since 1962**
- **Delivery Available** — large, contrasting color, one noticeable pop animation, then hold long enough to read

Delivery is a service offered by this fictional bakery. The heritage and ingredient statements are fictional sample claims, not verified claims about a real business.

## Sound

Use warm, light music underneath the narration, reduced while the voice speaks. Preserve a distinct opening thump before the music enters. Coordinate titles and narration so they complement each other.

## Production approach

1. Make a timed mockup with stills/placeholders, titles, temporary narration, and music before generating final video.
2. Review the opening impact, camera move, pastry choice, title placement, and closing-card emphasis in that mockup.
3. Build the closing card separately so text, colors, and animation can change without regenerating video footage.
4. Plan to use Nano Banana for complete reference scenes and Veo for motion. Start manually, then use the experience to plan automation.
5. Preserve consistent products, woman, setting, and branding across scenes. Strong reference stills should reduce retries, but video may still need multiple attempts.
6. Align the fictional homepage with the ad's final products, ingredients, delivery offer, and heritage before presenting the homepage-to-video example.
